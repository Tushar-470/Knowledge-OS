# MATHEMATICAL ATTACKS AND DEFENSES

## Q1: Why did you choose K=3 for Indomethacin? Is that the optimal number of K-Means clusters?
**Direct Answer:** K is not a K-Means cluster count.
**Technical Defense:** In PharmaPolySCOPE v2, K refers to the number of retained principal components in the PCA step. It is dynamically selected to satisfy a cumulative variance threshold. There is no clustering involved.
**Limitation:** The choice of cumulative variance threshold dictates K, which in turn defines the dimensionality of the evaluation subspace.
**If Examiner Pushes Further:** "We do not use K-Means, elbows, or silhouettes. K=3 means we retained 3 principal components to capture the required variance before applying SP-PRP-TOPSIS."
**Source Trace:** `asd_mcda/integration/pca.py`

## Q2: Why didn't you just use standard Euclidean TOPSIS instead of SP-PRP-TOPSIS?
**Direct Answer:** Standard Euclidean TOPSIS does not account for the covariance structure of the criteria.
**Technical Defense:** We use strictly SP-PRP-TOPSIS. We construct a metric tensor $M_K = V_K^T W V_K$ in the PCA subspace defined by the K retained components. This formulation correctly projects the weights and handles correlated inputs.
**Limitation:** This requires performing PCA first and operates in a reduced-dimensional space, which means some low-variance information is discarded.
**If Examiner Pushes Further:** "Standard Euclidean distance would double-count correlated criteria. The quadratic form distance using $M_K$ ensures orthogonal evaluation."
**Source Trace:** `asd_mcda/mcda/topsis.py`

## Q3: How do you define the ideal and anti-ideal solutions in your TOPSIS implementation?
**Direct Answer:** They are defined systematically using standardized bounds.
**Technical Defense:** In our SP-PRP-TOPSIS formulation, the ideal reference is defined as $z^+ = 1$ and the anti-ideal reference is defined as $z^- = 0$ for the standardized criteria in the projected space.
**Limitation:** These are theoretical bounds representing the mathematical maximum and minimum of the standardized space, not actual observed polymer profiles.
**If Examiner Pushes Further:** "The closeness coefficient is then strictly computed as $C_L = D^- / (D^+ + D^-)$, where $D^+$ and $D^-$ are the distances to these $z^+$ and $z^-$ references using the $M_K$ tensor."
**Source Trace:** `asd_mcda/mcda/topsis.py`

## Q4: Is your AHP Consistency Ratio (CR) of 0.08 a sign that your model is perfectly calibrated?
**Direct Answer:** No, it signifies mathematical coherence, not calibration.
**Technical Defense:** A CR < 0.08 means the pairwise comparison matrix generated by the decision-maker is mathematically consistent (i.e., minimal transitive contradictions).
**Limitation:** It does not evaluate whether the assigned weights actually correspond to physical reality or optimal formulation design.
**If Examiner Pushes Further:** "The CR diagnoses the eigenvectors of the judgment matrix; it is a check on human logic, not a validation of model accuracy."
**Source Trace:** `asd_mcda/mcda/ahp.py`

**Technical Defense:** Out of 10,000 total runs, 1,400 perturbed input vectors resulted in states that violated the defined mathematical boundary conditions of the framework (governance failures).
**Limitation:** These runs are discarded and not used in the final frequency calculations, meaning the analysis is conditioned only on the 8,600 valid mathematical states.
**If Examiner Pushes Further:** "The denominator for calculating rank frequencies is precisely $N_{valid} = 8,600$, not 10,000."

**Technical Defense:** We define $\sigma_{score}=0.05$ and $\sigma_{AHP}=0.15$ to apply synthetic noise during the Monte Carlo simulation. They test the stability of the SP-PRP-TOPSIS rankings mathematically.
**Limitation:** Because they are synthetic, they do not map to specific laboratory instrument errors or physical variances.

## Q7: How does Morris method variance decomposition prove your model works?
**Direct Answer:** The Morris method is not a variance decomposition method.
**Technical Defense:** Morris is a global screening method based on elementary effects. We compute $\mu^*$ (the mean absolute elementary effect) to rank parameter influence, and $\sigma$ to assess non-linearities or interactions.
**Limitation:** It provides a qualitative ranking of sensitivity, not a quantitative decomposition of total output variance (like Sobol indices would).
**If Examiner Pushes Further:** "We use it solely to screen for which inputs drive the TOPSIS $C_L$ score, not to decompose variance or prove physical laws."
**Source Trace:** `asd_mcda/uncertainty/morris.py`

## Q8: Does the closeness coefficient $C_L$ represent a probability?
**Direct Answer:** No, $C_L$ is a geometric relative distance metric.
**Technical Defense:** $C_L = D^- / (D^+ + D^-)$ calculates the relative closeness of a candidate to the ideal reference $z^+$, relative to the anti-ideal $z^-$, within the PCA subspace using the metric tensor $M_K$.
**Limitation:** It is strictly bounded between 0 and 1, but it is a dimensionless ranking score, not a statistical probability.
**If Examiner Pushes Further:** "A $C_L$ of 0.8 means the candidate is geometrically closer to the mathematical ideal than a candidate with 0.6, but it does not mean there is an 80% chance of experimental success."
**Source Trace:** `asd_mcda/mcda/topsis.py`

## Q9: How can you justify discarding the lower principal components in PCA? Doesn't that lose data?
**Direct Answer:** Yes, it discards low-variance data by design.
**Technical Defense:** We dynamically select K components to meet a specific cumulative variance threshold (e.g., 90%). The discarded components represent noise or very minor variations that do not significantly separate the alternatives.
**Limitation:** If critical decision information happens to lie in a low-variance direction, it will be lost in the SP-PRP-TOPSIS evaluation.
**If Examiner Pushes Further:** "The truncation to K components acts as a filter to stabilize the metric tensor $M_K$ and prevent overfitting to noise in the decision matrix."
**Source Trace:** `asd_mcda/integration/pca.py`

## Q10: What happens if your decision matrix contains missing data? Does the framework impute it?
**Direct Answer:** The framework requires a complete decision matrix; missing data causes governance failure.
**Technical Defense:** The mathematical operations, specifically the PCA projection and the SP-PRP-TOPSIS distance calculations, require dense vectors. Profiles with missing or inconsistent structural data (like DRG-0002) are quarantined.
**Limitation:** The framework cannot evaluate candidates with partial profiles.
**If Examiner Pushes Further:** "Imputation could introduce artificial mathematical artifacts that would corrupt the metric tensor $M_K$. Therefore, strict data governance is enforced."
